# Two Ways In — Bottom-Up and Top-Down

Alissa is designed to allow starting at either end of the Epistemic Stack. 

Neither approach is more correct or preferred, they serve different starting conditions and naturally converge toward the same integrated system.

## Bottom-Up: From Action to Alignment

_Start doing. Discover structure as you go._

This is the natural path for most users. You begin with what is immediately in front of you — things to do — and the system gently reveals its depth as your needs grow.

**The progression:**

```
1. TASKS           You have things to do. Create tasks, set a DoD, execute.
       ↓
2. DELEGATION      You can't do everything alone. Send a request, get
                   consent, transfer ownership. Discover accountability.
       ↓
3. BODIES OF WORK  Related tasks keep appearing. Group them under a
                   shared goal. Discover coordination.
       ↓
4. PROJECTS        The BOW needs a timeline, milestones, a sponsor.
                   Wrap it in a project. Discover delivery.
       ↓
5. OBJECTIVES      "What are we actually trying to achieve?"
                   Link the project to a measurable desired state.
       ↓
6. OBSERVABLES     "How do we know if it's working?"
       & TOCs       Define what to measure and why you believe
                   the intervention will move the needle.
```

**Why this works:** Each step is a natural response to a real need — not a mandatory onboarding sequence. A user who never progresses past Tasks still has a fully functional system. But when they hit the ceiling of flat task lists ("these tasks feel related — what's the bigger goal?"), the next layer is already there.

**Who enters this way:** Solo founders, individual contributors, new team members, anyone who thinks action-first and structure-later.

## Top-Down: From Strategy to Execution

_Know what you want. Design the path to get there._

This is the path for leaders, strategists, and anyone who begins with an outcome in mind and works backward to the tasks that will produce it.

**The progression:**

```
1. OBJECTIVE       "Revenue must increase by 20% this quarter."
   & OBSERVABLE    Define the metric. Set the target.
       ↓
2. THEORY OF       "We believe that reducing onboarding friction
   CHANGE          will increase conversion, which drives revenue."
                   Map the causal chain. State your hypotheses.
       ↓
3. PROJECT         Design the intervention. Set a timeline.
                   Name an owner, a sponsor, an outcome oneliner.
       ↓
4. MILESTONES      Break the project into time-bounded checkpoints.
                   Each milestone is a verifiable condition of progress.
       ↓
5. TASKS           Decompose each milestone into executable work.
                   Assign owners. Define DoDs. Delegate with consent.
       ↓
6. VALIDATE &      Execute, validate, re-observe. Did the observable
   RE-OBSERVE      shift? Was the theory right? Refine and repeat.
```

**Why this works:** The strategic user never has to "hope" their work ladders up to something meaningful. Every task is traceable to an objective from the moment it is created. When a task is validated, the system can evaluate whether the milestone progressed, whether the project is on track, and whether the underlying theory of change is gaining or losing confidence.

**Who enters this way:** Executives, program managers, strategic leaders, anyone who thinks outcome-first and decomposition-down.

## Convergence

Both paths lead to the same integrated system. The bottom-up user eventually discovers objectives and theories of change. The top-down user eventually delegates and watches tasks resolve. They meet in the middle — at the Project layer — where strategic intent and operational execution are joined.

```
       TOP-DOWN                              BOTTOM-UP

   Objective ◎ ←───── convergence ─────→ Task ☐
   Theory ⟁                               Delegation
   Project ◆  ◄══════════════════════►  BOW ▣
   Milestone ⬥                            Grouping
   Task ☐ ←──── same execution layer ───→ Objective ◎
```

This is by design. Alissa does not force you to start at the top or the bottom. It lets you enter where you are and grow toward where you need to be.
