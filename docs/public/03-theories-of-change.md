# Theories of Change — The Causal Worldview

**Symbol:** ⟁
**Question:** _What do we believe influences what?_
**Definition:** A reusable causal model describing structured beliefs about how changes in one or more observables influence other observables.

If observables are the vocabulary of reality, Theories of Change (TOCs) are the grammar — the rules we believe govern how one aspect of reality shapes another. A TOC is not a plan. It is not a goal. It is a **structured belief** about cause and effect.

## Why Theories of Change Matter

Every intervention carries an implicit causal model: _"We believe that if we do X, then Y will improve."_ Most organizations leave this belief implicit, untested, and unexamined. When the intervention fails, they cannot distinguish between a bad theory and bad execution.

Alissa makes the causal model explicit.

## Anatomy of a Theory of Change

A TOC is a named model containing:

- **Narrative** — A plain-language articulation of the causal worldview.
- **Scope** — Domain, market, team, or product area the theory applies to.
- **Hypotheses** — The causal influence edges (the heart of the model).
- **Assumptions** — Beliefs taken as given that underpin the model.

## Hypotheses: The Causal Edges

Each hypothesis represents a directed influence relationship between two observables:

```
[Source Observable] ──direction──▶ [Target Observable]
```

Every hypothesis is characterized along five dimensions:

| Dimension | Options | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | positive, negative, mixed, unknown | Does more X mean more or less Y? |
| **Effect Shape** | linear, saturating, threshold, step, inverted_u, delayed, exponential, conditional, unknown | What is the geometry of the influence? |
| **Mechanism** | _(free text, required)_ | **Why** — the causal reasoning |
| **Confidence** | assumed, weak, medium, strong, validated | How much evidence supports this? |
| **Expected Lag** | immediate, 1-7d, 1-4w, 1-3m, 3m+, unknown | How long until the effect manifests? |

## The Confidence Spectrum

TOC confidence is not binary. The system recognizes a progression:

- **Assumed** — Not tested, taken as given.
- **Weak** — Some indirect evidence.
- **Medium** — Reasonable evidence.
- **Strong** — Substantial evidence.
- **Validated** — Confirmed through measurement.

This progression is the engine of organizational learning. A TOC begins as an assumption and, through disciplined observation, either gains confidence or is revised.

## The Principle

> **TOCs reference observables, they do not define them. A TOC is a reusable model — it can underpin multiple projects and outlive any single intervention.**
